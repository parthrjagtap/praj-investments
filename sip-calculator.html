What is an SIP Calculator?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) calculator is an online tool that helps you estimate the future value of your mutual fund investments. Instead of doing complex math manually, you enter three simple inputs — monthly amount, expected return, and time period — and it instantly shows you the projected corpus. It's useful for both new investors just starting out and experienced ones stress-testing different scenarios.

How Does the SIP Calculator Work?

The calculator applies a compound interest formula to simulate how your monthly contributions grow over time. Here's what each input does:

1
Monthly SIP Amount — the fixed amount you invest every month
2
Expected Annual Return — based on historical fund performance; equity funds have typically delivered 10–14% over the long term
3
Investment Tenure — how many years you plan to stay invested; the longer, the more compounding works in your favour

The calculator also supports Lumpsum (one-time investment) and Step-up SIP (annual increase in contribution) modes — switch between tabs above.

The Formula

FV = P × [ ((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i ] × (1 + i)
FV Future value of the investment
P Monthly SIP amount
i Monthly rate of return (annual rate ÷ 12)
n Total number of instalments (months)
Example: ₹5,000/month · 12% p.a. · 7 years

Types of SIPs

Regular SIP

Fixed amount invested at regular intervals. Best for disciplined savers who want consistent, low-maintenance investing.

Step-up SIP

Automatically increases your SIP amount annually. Ideal for salaried professionals whose income grows over time — fights inflation and builds more wealth.

Flexible SIP

Contribution amount varies each month based on your income or market conditions. Great for people with irregular cash flows.

Perpetual SIP

No fixed end date — you stay invested indefinitely. Suited for long-term wealth builders with no specific exit timeline.

Trigger SIP

Invests only when market levels or NAV hits a pre-set threshold. For experienced investors who want to optimise entry points.

Multi SIP

Invest across multiple mutual fund schemes under a single SIP instruction. Simplifies management and enhances diversification.

SIP vs Lumpsum

Parameter SIP Lumpsum
Investment type Regular monthly contributions Single one-time investment
Market timing risk Lower — rupee cost averaging Higher — full amount exposed at once
Diversification Spread across multiple market cycles Limited if timed poorly
Best for Salaried investors, long-term wealth creation Those with surplus funds ready for higher risk

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Stopping SIPs during market dips

Market downturns are temporary. Pausing SIPs means missing lower NAVs — the best buying opportunity. Stay the course.

Not increasing contributions over time

As your income grows, your SIP should too. Use Step-up SIP to automatically scale up and beat inflation.

Investing without a goal

Random investing leads to misaligned outcomes. Define what you're saving for — retirement, education, home — and set your SIP accordingly.

Never reviewing fund performance

Markets change. Review your portfolio periodically and rebalance if a fund is consistently underperforming its benchmark.
